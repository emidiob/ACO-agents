# Curatorial research, exhibition logic and delivery

Original ACO operational method · v0.6.0. Select for the relevant task; do not load every playbook.

## Brief and authority
Clarify the question the exhibition investigates, who is commissioning it, available work and venue conditions. Do not confuse the curator's concept with the artist's consent. Work selection, interpretation, display changes, fees and rights require the relevant human decisions.

## Research before selection
Construct a short argument with open questions and possible counterexamples. Research existing works and contexts using original collection records, artist sources and critical writing. Record why each source is useful and what it cannot establish. Repeated participation in famous exhibitions is not a curatorial argument. Do not claim to have visited a venue or watched a work that was not available.

## Selection and relationships
For each work, record its proposed function in the exhibition, material requirements, relation to neighboring works, loan/commission status and open rights/fee questions. Avoid making all works illustrations of the same thesis. Identify disagreement, rhythm, scale and transitions; retain the possibility that a work should not be included. Proposed selection is not artist agreement or confirmed availability.

## Spatial and temporal score
Work from an actual floor plan when available. Map entry, sightlines, circulation, pauses, exits, sound spill, projection light, running times, captions, seating and accessible alternatives. State dimensions as unverified when no plan is supplied. For media work, distinguish the loop length from expected visitor dwell time. Artistic intention does not waive electrical, rigging, fire or safeguarding requirements; qualified venue staff own those approvals.

## Public interpretation
Write one concise proposition, a visitor-facing entry text and a work-level sample appropriate to the brief. Avoid explanations that merely repeat the artist statement. Attribute quotations, flag translations and keep sponsor messaging distinct from curatorial interpretation. Plan public activities only when they deepen the exhibition and have realistic hosts, access and resources.

## Production handoff
Supply an annotated work list, proposed sequence, installation dependencies, budget categories, rights questions and an approval matrix. Include deinstallation, return, preservation and documentation responsibilities. Fees and insurance are not covered merely because a venue has agreed in principle. Exhibition producers translate approved choices into executable schedules and technical riders.

## QA and memory
Can every inclusion be argued beyond shared subject matter? Are loans still provisional? Can the show be installed safely and accessed meaningfully? Is consent for every significant display change recorded? Keep proposals inside the curator/organization pipeline; an exhibition folder is not created for each unaccepted concept. Store only a concise decision and references in ACO.md; actual plans and loan documents retain their own originals.
