# Directing: scene purpose, blocking and coverage

## Scope
Use film language to serve an intention, not to decorate prompts with adjectives. Resolve the story or communication objective, duration/deliverables and production constraints first. A single-shot prompt does not need a full agency workshop.

## Direction procedure
1. State what changes for the viewer or character by the end of the scene. Identify what the viewer knows at entry and the intended reveal at exit.
2. Divide the scene into observable beats: action, reaction, decision, reveal or transition. Assign a purpose to every shot; remove redundant coverage before spending generation or shoot budget.
3. Establish spatial geography and actor/object positions. Define the action axis, eyelines and screen directions. Crossing the axis or breaking continuity can be intentional, but record the reason and orienting shot.
4. Direct performance through playable behaviour, timing, attention and relationships, not only abstract emotion. Keep physical actions achievable within the clip length.
5. Choose camera height, distance, framing, movement and approximate lens intent as a coherent plan. State whether the camera or subject moves; avoid contradictory simultaneous instructions.
6. Plan essential coverage for the intended edit: establishing context, action, reaction, inserts and connective material as appropriate. Do not require every conventional shot when an intentional long take works better.
7. Convert into numbered storyboard panels with reference frames, start/end state, movement arrows described in text, audio and edit purpose. A textual board is not an image file; use an authorized image tool only when actual frames are requested.
8. Build an animatic or frame-accurate timing table before expensive production. Include pauses, sound cues and transition overlaps in the total duration.
9. Review story comprehension, timing and production feasibility. Offer a simpler route when generation limits conflict with the shot; don't promise exact complex choreography from one prompt.

## Deliverable
Short treatment plus shot list, continuity notes, production/generation assumptions and an editable timing plan. For artist-led work, conceptual decisions stay with the artist; commercial or technical staff do not silently change the work.

## Claims boundary
No invented on-set experience or claims of having directed/shot/rendered assets. Named filmmaker references are optional contextual references, not a substitute for explicit framing, blocking and sound decisions.
