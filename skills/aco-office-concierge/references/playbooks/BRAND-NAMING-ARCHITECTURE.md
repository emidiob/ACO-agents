# Naming and multi-brand architecture

Original ACO operational method · v0.6.0. Select for the relevant task; do not load every playbook.

## Architecture before proliferation
Map the real legal/operating organization, offers, audiences and existing brand equity. Distinguish legal company, activity, public brand, product and client. Evaluate whether a descriptive activity label is enough before proposing a new brand or company. An exploratory service remains a section/pipeline item; a naming session does not create a business entity.

## Naming brief
Clarify the languages, markets, desired associations, what must be avoided, practical length and relationship to existing names. Generate distinct territories with reasons, not synonym batches. Test pronunciation, spelling, ambiguity, category associations and use across the actual applications. Flag potentially adverse meanings for review by competent local speakers; do not claim linguistic clearance across languages you have not checked.

## Screening
Separate creative shortlist from preliminary public search and formal legal clearance. Search dates, queries and jurisdictions matter. An available domain, handle or company name does not establish trademark availability. Do not register anything or contact an existing name/handle owner without authorization. Escalate trademark advice to qualified counsel with the shortlist and intended goods/services, rather than issuing a guarantee.

## Architecture choices
Explain the operational consequences of parent/sub-brand, endorsed or independent identities in plain language. Which promise is shared? What must remain separate? Who approves use? How will invoices, websites and collaborator credits remain unambiguous? Consider transition costs and legacy links before advocating a rebrand.

## Output and retention
Give a short rationale-led shortlist, architecture sketch, screening limitations and the decision required. Retain selected names and status in the canonical Brand section. Rejected candidates can be a short note, not separate documents. A proposal is not an approved name or a cleared mark.
