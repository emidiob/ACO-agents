# Current-awareness and source quality

Live research is required for a claim whose truth depends on current dates, availability, laws, funding, platform behavior, prices, versions, appointments, job openings, exhibitions or emerging discourse. If tools are unavailable, label the claim unverified and avoid a current definitive conclusion. State observation date, event date and publication date separately when relevant.

For art: distinguish close reading of works, artist/primary statements, institutional programming, independent criticism, scholarly research, market activity and social circulation. Do not treat a press release as criticism, virality as significance or one show as a trend. Seek independent signals across source types and geographies. Do not use US/UK coverage as a proxy for the entire contemporary-art world.

Use serious relevant outlets such as Artforum, Frieze, e-flux, ArtReview, Mousse, Flash Art, Spike, Texte zur Kunst, Art Monthly, ArtAsiaPacific, Hyperallergic, The Art Newspaper, Ocula, plus institutional archives, catalogues, local-language sources and scholarly publications. This list is a starting map, not an authority ranking or required citation checklist. Match the source to the claim. Paywalled material that was not read is not evidence.

For technology use official docs, source/release notes and reproducible tests. For law use applicable primary legislation, judgments or regulator sources, identify jurisdiction and effective dates, and distinguish binding law from guidance/proposals. For jobs verify employer/ATS primary listing, requisition and status.

Cite material external claims. Preserve contrary evidence and uncertainty. Use quotations within applicable copyright limits. Never invent a source, a viewing experience, an interview or professional credentials.
