# Visual language for ACO briefs

Name the visible or requested effect. Treat focal length, film stock and camera hardware as optional design choices unless documented; do not infer exact equipment from appearance.

## Camera clauses

| Intent | Useful wording |
|---|---|
| Stable composition | Camera holds its position and framing throughout the action |
| Pan | Camera rotates toward the subject as it crosses the room |
| Tilt | Camera tilts upward when the object rises above the subject's shoulders |
| Physical approach | Camera moves closer; foreground and background shift relative to each other |
| Zoom | Field of view tightens from the same camera position |
| Track | Camera travels alongside the moving subject, maintaining its distance |
| Orbit | Camera moves around the subject through a specified angle and direction |
| Focus change | Focus passes from the foreground object to the person behind it at the stated event |
| Handheld | Small camera movements follow the operator's stance; specify intensity only if needed |

Specify framing separately: close view of hands, waist-up view, full figure or wide environment. State camera height and viewing direction when they determine the image. Do not combine incompatible instructions such as an unchanged frame and an unexplained orbit.

## Appearance and character briefs

Describe features needed for continuity: silhouette, hair, visible facial traits, garment shapes/colors, shoes when visible and carried objects. Keep pose and expression in the action layer. A back view should remain a back view even when a front-facing identity image is supplied.

Specify illumination through direction, softness, contrast and the visible source where established. Material descriptions should distinguish behavior: soft fabric folds, rigid edges, reflective metal or translucent glass. Surface texture and ordinary variation are useful when visible; arbitrarily adding blemishes or damage changes the design.

For still-image preparation, write: intended image use, composition, subject/design, environment, illumination and the required difference from the supplied reference. If only one detail is wrong, request an edit limited to that detail rather than redesigning the entire anchor. In an approved reference set, check that all images depict compatible materials, scale and illumination.
