# Concept and visual materials (ACO original; Hallmark studied)

Use the ACO catalog of **50 original material-direction records** (`assets/directions.json`). These are idea/typography/space/color/image behavior combinations **not Hallmark's copyrighted screenshot assets or exact original themes**. A theme is material, not a ready-made layout: content determines the page/film/print structure.

### Explore
- Inventory 8–12 facts unique to this project (product, place, material, ritual, language, object, imagery). Do not invent data, awards, claims, customers or usable photos.
- Derive 2–3 distinct **composition devices** from those facts. An empty style moodboard is not an idea. Test with a real competitor substitution: could the idea describe its page unchanged?
- Compare typography hierarchy, grid, image role, rhythm, negative space, color, scale and density. Explain the trade-offs, not simply “premium” or “modern”.
- Keep approved tokens and the true copy. If content doesn't support a proposed component, change the composition rather than fabricate facts.
- The direction selector offers variety, not objective quality. Human or qualified design approval is contextual; a scripted score does not replace art direction.

### Anti-template evaluation
Flag repeated centered statement + three cards + decorative hero, generic invented metrics, purposeless gradients, fake product windows, stock typography and content-free oversized whitespace **as hypotheses**. Retain any such choice when it serves the actual subject. Test real viewports and states; do not impose one anti-slop aesthetic across the entire portfolio.

### Source evaluation
Hallmark has a larger upstream runtime, theme library and browser scanner. ACO's original 50-direction concept selector plus conservative static checks **does not claim execution parity** with those scripts. Existing ACO Webdesign's 62 design-review rules remain primary for executable web audits.

CLI: `taste.py directions --brief BRIEF.json`; `taste.py audit-css --file FILE.css`; `taste.py audit-html --file FILE.html`.
