# Subject-led art direction (ACO original; Anthropic frontend/canvas references)

## For digital interfaces
- Find one visual proposition with evidence from the subject, not an automatic category moodboard. Preserve the audience's main task.
- Give type a compositional role: measured line length, clear hierarchy, scale contrast, fitting font permissions. Labels/borders must encode something.
- Use imagery with a factual relationship to the subject; the actual product screenshot, genuine photograph or user's assets usually beats decoration.
- Preserve tone and interaction meaning. Responsive states are part of the composition; no “desktop poster scaled down”.
- Produce a compact direction contract before coding: distinctive anchor, primary action, locked tokens, grid/text/image strategy, assets, accepted compromises, accessibility.

## For static graphics, photography and print
- Formulate the visual philosophy in terms of material, spatial relationships, scale, contrast, rhythm, color and typography; avoid relying on a checklist of universal stylistic prohibitions.
- Make the intended hierarchy legible at final output size, including crop marks, bleed and image density where relevant.
- Use user-supplied or authorized imagery; do not reconstruct copyrighted pieces or place unlicensed fonts in archives.
- Critique the **actual generated still/page**, not a prose description of it.

## Acceptance
The design must communicate something specific about the subject, preserve facts and brand where locked, and survive the rendered artifact's constraints. Art direction is a professional assessment, not an automatic score.
